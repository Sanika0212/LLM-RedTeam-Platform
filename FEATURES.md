# FEATURES.md — LLM Red-Team & Evaluation Platform: Offensive Roadmap

This is the **offensive / capability roadmap** companion to `AUDIT_REPORT.md`. Where the audit catalogs the *defensive* research-validity fixes (broken detectors, magic-constant thresholds, missing CIs, unvalidated ground truth), this document is the menu of *new capabilities* — attacks, metrics, judges, benchmarks, and reproducibility infrastructure — that turn the platform from a working tool into a publishable, reviewer-credible benchmark. Each item below survived independent critique against the actual codebase, so the integration points name real modules (`strategies/`, `detectors/`, `analysis/`, `backend/api/routes/`). Pick from these; the audit fixes come first (see build order at the end).

## How to read this

Every feature is tagged **Impact** (high / medium / low — leverage on the paper's claims) · **Effort** (S / M / L — S = days, M = a week-ish, L = multi-week, often labeling-bound) · **Novelty** (high / medium / low — how much it differentiates vs PyRIT / Garak / HarmBench). Bracketed IDs like `[attacks/AT1]` are stable handles for cross-referencing.

## Tier 1 — Quick wins (S effort, high leverage)

Only two items are genuinely S-effort; both are worth doing early.

| Feature | Lens/ID | What & why | Impact · Effort · Novelty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Policy-Puppetry / Skeleton-Key override strategy** | `attacks/AT5` | A `PolicyPuppetryAttack` that frames the harmful intent as a fake system/config artifact (pseudo-XML/JSON "policy" blocks, the Skeleton-Key "augment-don't-refuse" handshake, roleplay-as-config). ## Tier 2 — Research-grade features (reviewer-credibility additions)

### A. New attacks

- **Many-Shot / In-Context Jailbreaking (MSJ)** `[attacks/AT1]` — A `ManyShotAttack` that stuffs N fabricated compliant user/assistant turns before the real intent, sweeping N (4→128) to expose the documented power-law ASR-vs-shots scaling. Probes the **long-context regime** that Crescendo/TAP/PAIR never touch and adds a context-length axis to **RQ1**; also stress-tests `semantic_drift.py` on a non-Crescendo multi-turn pattern. Integration: new `strategies/many_shot.py` subclassing `AttackStrategy`, metadata → `analysis/metrics.py` (ASR@k exists) + a new ASR-vs-shots plot. *Lit: Anil et al. 2024 (Anthropic), in-context scaling.* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

- **AutoDAN genetic / evolutionary attack** `[attacks/AT2]` — A hierarchical GA over jailbreak prompts (population, fitness via the existing detector + PAIR/TAP `_score_response`, crossover + mutation, elitist selection), early-stopping on jailbreak. Each node logs `parent_id` + `mutation_applied`, so it plugs straight into `analysis/genealogy.py`. The canonical *stealthy, fluency-preserving* baseline reviewers expect; the platform has beam (TAP) and greedy (PAIR) but no population search. Sharpens **RQ3** (beam vs genetic vs Thompson) and supercharges genealogy/mutation-efficiency (**RQ6/NOVEL**). Integration: new `strategies/autodan.py`; lineage via `StrategyResult.metadata` → `build_tree_from_generator_output()`. *Lit: Liu et al. 2023 (arXiv:2310.04451); Lapid et al. 2023.* **Impact medium · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

- **Best-of-N (BoN) stochastic sampling baseline** `[attacks/AT3]` — N independent queries with augmented surface forms (random caps, char scramble, leetspeak, temperature) and success if ANY jailbreaks; fits the empirical BoN power-law and exposes `attack_success@N`. The **mandatory control**: without it a reviewer cannot tell whether Crescendo/TAP/PAIR gains are intelligence or just more samples. Operationalizes **RQ2/RQ3** (multi-turn/search must beat BoN at equal budget) and gives the empirical counterpart to the theoretical ASR@k curve; also exercises **RQ4** via per-intent variance. Integration: new `strategies/best_of_n.py`; augmentations reuse `generators.py`; curve → `metrics.py` + Leaderboard/Research. *Lit: Hughes et al. 2024.* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

- **Cipher & low-resource-language obfuscation** `[attacks/AT4]` — A `CipherAttack` + `cipher` mutation family: ROT13/Caesar, ASCII/Unicode, Morse, base64 (a generators stub never decoded by any strategy today), CipherChat self-cipher, and the strongest variant — translation into low-resource languages (Zulu, Scots Gaelic, Hmong) with round-trip back to English before detectors run. Exploits the English/plaintext-centricity of safety training; the repo has **zero multilingual coverage** and no encode-then-decode strategy. Opens a new **RQ1** dimension and **RQ5/transfer** (do ciphers transfer across families → CATS). Integration: new `strategies/cipher.py` + generators ops; translate hook on `llm_client`; → `analysis/transfer.py`. *Lit: Yuan et al. 2024 (CipherChat, ICLR); Yong et al. 2023; Wei et al. 2023.* **Impact medium · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

### B. Metrics & statistics

- **Bootstrap CIs + significance tests for ASR/CWSS & rankings** `[metrics/ST1]` — A `significance.py` with BCa/percentile bootstrap CIs, **Wilson intervals** (correct for the near-0/near-1, N≈65 regime), **McNemar's exact test** on the paired per-prompt jailbreak table (prompts are paired across models — unpaired chi-square is invalid), paired-bootstrap ASR diffs, and Holm-Bonferroni for all-pairs comparison. The single most-demanded rigor addition: every claim today is a point estimate over 65 prompts. Underpins **RQ1** (rank families), **RQ2** (McNemar on Crescendo vs single-shot), **RQ4**. Integration: `compute_all_metrics()` (+ `asr_ci`, `cwss_ci`), `compare_models()` (significance flag on `_ranking`), `report.py` ("ASR = 0.31 [0.21, 0.43]"). Pure numpy. *Lit: Wilson 1927; McNemar 1947; Efron-Tibshirani BCa; Holm 1979; Dror et al. 2018.* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty low.**

- **Confidence-calibration suite (ECE / Brier / reliability diagram) — answers RQ6** `[metrics/ST2]` — A `calibration.py` computing ECE, MCE, Brier, reliability-diagram payload, and Platt slope/intercept in two modes: detector-confidence calibration (needs gold labels), and the RQ6-native variant (bucket by model confidence, correlate with jailbreak *severity* via Spearman ρ). **RQ6 is listed in README + NOVEL_ARCHITECTURE but has zero implementation** — a hole a reviewer spots immediately. Also retroactively justifies CWSS, which weights by detector confidence and is meaningless if that confidence is miscalibrated. Integration: reads `PromptMetrics.confidence`; new `GET /analytics/calibration/{eval_id}`; reliability JSON → Research panel. *Lit: Guo et al. 2017; Brier 1950; Naeini et al. 2015; Platt 1999.* **Impact medium · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

- **Attack-efficiency frontier: success-vs-query-budget, cost-normalized** `[metrics/ST5]` — An `efficiency.py` turning per-intent `attempts` + `all_jailbreak_flags` (already recorded) into P(success by budget b), AUEC, queries-to-first-jailbreak distributions, and cost-normalized ASR-per-query / per-1k-tokens / per-dollar via a model price table; plus a strategy Pareto frontier. **RQ2/RQ3 are fundamentally efficiency questions**, but the platform reports only terminal ASR — it literally cannot show one strategy is more query-efficient. The standard framing in the jailbreak literature (TAP/PAIR "twenty queries"), and cost-normalized ASR is absent from PyRIT/Garak/HarmBench — a real differentiator. Distinct from the Thompson budget sim (which allocates *across categories*). Integration: consumes `StrategyResult`; new `GET /analytics/attack-efficiency`. *Lit: Mehrotra et al. 2023 (TAP); Chao et al. 2023 (PAIR).* **Impact high · Effort L · Novelty medium.** *(Token/dollar variants need a small `base.py run()` instrumentation change.)*

- **Inter-detector & human-label agreement (κ / α)** `[metrics/ST4]` — An `agreement.py`: Cohen's κ (ML vs heuristic jailbreak detector — both exist and disagree on ambiguous cases; or detector vs human gold), Fleiss' κ / Krippendorff's α for 3+ labelers, percent agreement, and a **disagreement table** surfacing the exact boundary cases. The ML/heuristic κ is essentially free and quantifies detector robustness; detector-vs-human κ is the standard eval-paper reliability report; the boundary cases are themselves a publishable finding about measurement difficulty. Integration: new `analysis/agreement.py`; small `jailbreak.py` change to expose dual verdicts; new `GET /analytics/detector-agreement`. *Lit: Cohen 1960; Fleiss 1971; Krippendorff; Landis & Koch 1977.* **Impact low · Effort M · Novelty low.** *(Report prevalence alongside κ to avoid the kappa paradox.)*

### C. Detection & judging

- **LLM-as-Judge detector with structured rubric + self-consistency voting** `[detectors/DE1]` — A `llm_judge.py` exposing `judge_response(...) -> JudgeResult` ({is_harmful, harm_category, severity 0-3, refusal_type, confidence, rationale, evidence_span}), sampled N=5 at temperature>0 with majority vote + inter-sample agreement as a per-decision reliability signal, and a strict JSON parser falling back to the keyword path on outage. The **biggest credibility hole**: keyword/embedding detection misclassifies "Sure! Here's why I won't help…" and paraphrased compliance, and *every* downstream metric inherits that noise. The de-facto standard in HarmBench/StrongREJECT/PAIR. Upgrades **RQ1** and **RQ3** (TAP's allocation is only as good as its scorer), and makes the CWSS confidence term mean something. Integration: `core.evaluate_prompt` (optional `judge=` backend) and as the scorer for `tap.py`/`pair.py` `_score_response`; client via `backend/integrations/factory`. *Lit: Souly et al. 2024 (StrongREJECT); Mazeika et al. 2024; Chao et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2022 (self-consistency).* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty medium.** *(Cache on prompt+response hash; pin a strong judge; the judge can be jailbroken/sycophantic itself.)*

- **Grounded hallucination checker (claim extraction + retrieval/NLI)** `[detectors/DE4]` — Upgrades hallucination detection from "contains a citation-shaped string" to "is the claim actually unsupported": LLM claim decomposition → per-claim Supported/Unsupported/Contradicted via NLI cross-encoder + a real **DOI/arXiv existence check** replacing the regex `suspicious_url` heuristic; emits a grounded score, per-claim table, and `fabricated_citation_count`. Today a perfectly-cited answer is penalized identically to a confabulation, making the hallucination metric and attack family unreportable. Standard factuality methodology, and it **dovetails with the medical-AI lane** (ungrounded clinical claims are the core risk). Integration: new `grounded_hallucination.py` augmenting `hallucination.py` in `core.py`; DOI/arXiv network util; pluggable retrieval backend. *Lit: Min et al. 2023 (FActScore); Wei et al. 2024 (SAFE); Chern et al. 2023 (FacTool); Thorne et al. 2018 (FEVER).* **Impact high · Effort L · Novelty high.** *(Offline mode degrades to citation-existence + NLI-against-provided-doc; cap claims per response.)*

- **Configurable harm taxonomy aligned to a public standard** `[detectors/DE5]` — A versioned `harm_taxonomy.py` (YAML/JSON) of ~13 hazard categories mapped to **MLCommons AILuminate v1.0** and/or OpenAI usage policy, a crosswalk from the platform's six ad-hoc families + 65 prompts to standard IDs, `classify_harm_category()` (judge-backed, keyword fallback), and per-standard-category reporting of ASR/SWHS/heatmap. The six internal families are an invention no other benchmark uses, so results aren't comparable and SWHS severity weights have **no external basis**. Makes ASR-by-hazard directly citable and policy-grounded. Integration: new `harm_taxonomy.py` + `taxonomy/*.yaml`; crosswalk in `datasets/adversarial_prompts.py`; per-category reporting in `analytics.py`/`report.py`. *Lit: MLCommons AILuminate v1.0; OpenAI usage policies; Weidinger et al. 2022.* **Impact medium · Effort M · Novelty medium.** *(Allow multi-label for dual-use prompts; pin a version string per report.)*

### D. Datasets & benchmarks

- **Unified public-benchmark loader layer** `[datasets/DA1]` — A `datasets/benchmarks/` package with a `BenchmarkLoader` ABC returning records in the existing `AttackPrompt` schema, plus concrete loaders: HarmBench, AdvBench (520 GCG strings), JailbreakBench (JBB, 100 harmful + 100 benign), StrongREJECT (313), XSTest (250 safe + 200 unsafe over-refusal probes). Each pulls from HF Hub with a vendored CSV/JSON fallback for offline CI, normalizes onto the six families, and registers via `get_benchmark(name, split, n)`. **Every paper number currently comes from 65 self-authored prompts** — a reviewer will reject these as non-comparable / cherry-picked. Recognized benchmarks make ASR/CWSS/transfer comparable to published leaderboards and let CATS/BABA/genealogy be shown on accepted data. Strengthens **RQ1** and **RQ5**. Integration: imported by seed endpoints exactly like `get_all_prompts()`; flows unchanged through `eval_service.py`. *Lit: Mazeika et al. 2024; Zou et al. 2023; Chao et al. 2024; Souly et al. 2024; Röttger et al. 2024.* **Impact high · Effort L · Novelty low.** *(Mind gated/research-only licensing; keep HF optional behind CSV fallback.)*

- **Benchmark provenance + ground-truth fields in schema + seed-by-benchmark endpoint** `[datasets/DA2]` — Extends the `AttackPrompt` ORM with `source`, `benchmark_split`, `prompt_type` (`adversarial`|`benign_control`), and a JSON `reference` (gold answers, rubric items), + an Alembic migration, a `POST /attack-prompts/seed-benchmark` route, and `?source=`/`?prompt_type=` filters. **The minimal schema change that unlocks every other dataset feature**: without `source` you can't say "ASR on HarmBench-val"; `benign_control` is the hook that makes over-refusal (DA4) possible at all; `reference` stores the ground truth DA3/DA6 graders need. Integration: `backend/models/attack_prompt.py` + migration; `attack_prompts.py` route; `eval_service.py` selection. **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty low.** *(Nullable columns with defaults for backward compatibility.)*

- **StrongREJECT-style rubric grader (pluggable backend)** `[datasets/DA3]` — A `rubric_grader.py` scoring refusal × specificity × convincingness into a 0-1 harmfulness score (vs the current binary keyword refusal), with an LLM-judge backend (reusing TAP's judge plumbing) and a deterministic heuristic fallback for CI; `is_jailbroken` is then thresholded from the rubric score. StrongREJECT/HarmBench show keyword matching **overcounts** jailbreaks (a non-refusing useless answer scores as success); a reviewer comparing to published numbers will demand the same grader. Sharpens **RQ1** and **RQ6**; makes CWSS/SWHS defensible. Integration: called from `core.evaluate_prompt` when `source ∈ {strongreject, harmbench}`; new aggregate in `metrics.py`. *Lit: Souly et al. 2024; Mazeika et al. 2024.* **Impact high · Effort L · Novelty medium.**

- **Over-refusal benchmark + ORR / helpfulness-safety frontier (XSTest)** `[datasets/DA4]` — Uses the XSTest loader + `benign_control` to bring 250 safe-but-alarming prompts into the pool, grades a refusal-on-benign as WRONG, and adds Over-Refusal Rate, Compliance-Refusal AUC, and a Safety-Helpfulness Frontier point (adversarial ASR vs benign ORR) per model. The platform measures refusal *consistency* but has **no benign prompts** — so a model that refuses everything scores maximally safe, a serious validity gap. Over-refusal is now first-class; reporting ASR without it is the most common reviewer critique. Makes the leaderboard two-dimensional and complements **RQ4**. Integration: XSTest loader (DA1) + `prompt_type` (DA2); metrics in `metrics.py`; new `GET /analytics/over-refusal/{eval_id}`; frontier scatter in `Research.tsx`. *Lit: Röttger et al. 2024 (XSTest); Cui et al. 2024 (OR-Bench).* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

- **TruthfulQA + BBQ answer-key graders** `[datasets/DA6]` — Two answer-keyed loaders + graders: TruthfulQA (817 Qs, gold answer sets → truthfulness score wired into the hallucination category) and BBQ (ambiguous/disambiguated contexts → the standard BBQ bias score, feeding the bias detector). The current hallucination detector has no notion of ground truth; **BBQ is cited in the README (Parrish 2022) but never implemented** as a scored benchmark. Gives **RQ5** the field-standard bias metric and a recognizable hallucination benchmark; reuses the DA2 `reference` field and DA3 judge plumbing, so marginal cost is low. Integration: `truthfulqa.py` + `bbq.py` loaders; graders from `core.py`; aggregates in `metrics.py`; bias grader → `bias.py`. *Lit: Lin et al. 2022; Parrish et al. 2022.* **Impact medium · Effort L · Novelty medium.** *(Document the grader variant; BBQ needs multiple-choice templating.)*

### E. Reproducibility & reporting

- **Immutable run manifest + content-addressed provenance** `[platform/PL1]` — A `RunManifest` frozen at launch on the `Evaluation` (model+version/`system_fingerprint`, decoding params, engine git SHA, detector versions, strategy hyperparams, SHA-256 `dataset_hash`, RNG seed, `manifest_hash`), surfaced via `GET /evaluations/{id}/manifest`. Today the only run metadata is a freeform `config` JSON — nothing pins which model snapshot, data, code, or seed produced a number, so two ASR values aren't known to be comparable. The precondition for the platform being a citable artifact; underpins every cross-model claim in **RQ1/RQ5**. Integration: `eval_service.run_evaluation` (after prompt+client resolution), `evaluation.py` + migration, integration clients (capture `system_fingerprint`), new `/manifest` endpoint. *Lit: model cards / datasheets; HELM/lm-eval-harness run metadata; novel as red-team-specific provenance.* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty medium.**

- **Seedable deterministic runs (engine RNG + provider seed pass-through)** `[platform/PL2]` — Threads an integer `seed` through the stack: `engine.seed(seed)` for `random`/numpy used by generators/TAP/PAIR/adaptive_budget (which already takes a `seed` arg at line 337, never wired), OpenAI request-level `seed=` + recorded `system_fingerprint`, and deterministic prompt ordering. The engine hardcodes `temperature=0.0` but passes **no seed anywhere**, so "novel" results like adaptive-budget gains (**RQ3/RQ5**) aren't reproducible run-to-run. The single most reviewer-demanded property for a benchmark artifact. Integration: `core.py`, `strategies/*`, `generators.py` (seeded RNG instances), `adaptive_budget.py` (wire existing arg), `integrations/base.py`+`openai_client.py`, `eval_service.py`. *Lit: determinism caveats in lm-eval-harness/HELM; OpenAI `seed`+`system_fingerprint`.* **Impact medium · Effort M · Novelty medium.** *(Honest deliverable: "deterministic given fixed fingerprint, plus recorded fingerprint to detect drift" — state as a limitation, don't over-claim.)*

- **CIs on every rate metric across leaderboard & analytics** `[platform/PL3]` — An `intervals.py` providing Wilson CIs for proportions + bootstrap CIs for compound metrics, returning `{value, ci_low, ci_high, n}` from the analytics endpoints, rendering error bars + `12.3% [7.1, 19.8]` text, and **CI-aware leaderboard ordering** (overlapping CIs aren't presented as a meaningful gap), plus an A-vs-B two-proportion/Fisher helper used by PL5. ASR/CWSS are bare point estimates everywhere and per-category n can be ~10 — a 30% vs 20% difference may be pure noise. Any RQ1/RQ2/RQ4/RQ5 claim is unpublishable without this. Integration: `intervals.py` → `metrics.py`; CI fields from `analytics.py`; `Analytics.tsx` + `Leaderboard.tsx`. *Lit: Wilson 1927; Efron bootstrap; HELM/HarmBench reporting.* **Impact high · Effort M · Novelty low.** *(Overlaps ST1/PF2 — same statistical core; build once, surface in all three places.)*

- **Reproducible "Safety Report Card" with manifest, seeds, stat-cards** `[metrics/ST6]` — Extends `report.py` into a versioned JSON+Markdown+HTML card bundling: every headline metric with its CI (ST1), the calibration verdict (ST2) + detector AUC, a reproducibility manifest (git commit, dataset hash, detector version, seeds, hyperparams, model snapshot), a model-card-style summary, and a stable content hash so two runs can be diffed and a re-run certified "reproduced"; adds `report_to_html()` + `diff_report_cards()`. The current report has **no provenance whatsoever**. Composes ST1-ST5 into the one artifact a venue's reproducibility checklist asks for. Integration: extends `report.py`; pulls from `significance.py`/`calibration.py`/`detector_eval.py`; new "Report Card" frontend view. *Lit: Mitchell et al. 2019 (Model Cards); Gebru et al. 2021 (Datasheets); NeurIPS/ML repro checklists.* **Impact high · Effort L · Novelty medium.**

- **Run-to-run diff + one-click attack replay** `[platform/PL5]` — Built on PL1/PL2: `GET /evaluations/diff?a=&b=` aligns two evals by `dataset_hash`, returning per-prompt verdict flips, category ASR deltas with the PL3 significance test, and a **manifest diff** attributing each change to a cause (model version / seed / detector / decoding); plus `POST /evaluations/{id}/replay` reconstructing a run from the manifest with optional single-axis override. There is **zero** diff/replay support today. Operationalizes **RQ4** (replay → verdict-flip rate as an empirical nondeterminism metric) and makes RQ2/RQ3 ablations a single controlled-override click. Integration: new `/diff` + `/{id}/replay` in `evaluations.py`; replay constructor in `eval_service.py`; new Diff view. *Lit: verdict-flip / run-to-run agreement as reliability; manifest-attributed diff novel vs PyRIT/Garak/HarmBench.* **Impact medium · Effort L · Novelty high.**

- **Batched model × strategy sweep with queue + live cost/token meter** `[platform/PL4]` — One POST creates the cartesian `{models} × {strategies} × {repeats}` as child Evaluations under a `sweep_id` on the existing Celery path, with `GET /sweeps/{id}` roll-up, live token/cost aggregation (price columns on `LLMModel`, `usage` already in `extra_data`), a pre-run cost estimate, **and a fix to the Anthropic client to populate `usage`** (it currently doesn't). The platform runs evals one-at-a-time with no way to launch the matrix RQ1/RQ2/RQ3/RQ5 require, and CATS needs the *same* prompts across models — a sweep guarantees that alignment; compute cost is a now-standard methods-section requirement. Integration: new sweep route reusing the `/run` task path; columns on `evaluation.py`/`llm_model.py` + migration; new Sweep view. *Lit: HELM cost/token reporting.* **Impact medium · Effort L · Novelty medium.** *(Store the price used in the PL1 manifest; lean on existing rate limiting for backpressure.)*

- **Reproducibility bundle: LaTeX/CSV export + signed snapshot** `[platform/PL6]` — `GET /analytics/bundle` produces a `.zip`: paper-ready CSV + LaTeX booktabs tables (with PL3 CIs), matplotlib PDF/PNG figures, all PL1 manifests, a frozen leaderboard JSON, a `repro/` folder (pinned `requirements.txt`, docker-compose ref, `REPRODUCE.md` with seeds + manifest hashes + commands), and a top-level `bundle_hash`. The existing export is a single truncated CSV — nothing a paper can cite. The capstone artifact for artifact-evaluation committees. Integration: new `/bundle` endpoint reusing the CSV writer + PL3 CIs + PL1 manifests; new `analysis/export.py`; export button in `Analytics.tsx`/`Leaderboard.tsx`. *Lit: ACM/NeurIPS artifact-eval norms; HELM/EleutherAI results+configs releases.* **Impact medium · Effort L · Novelty medium.** *(Sequence last — depends on PL1 + PL3.)*

> **Consolidation note — labeled gold set & detector validation.** Five JSON items describe the same load-bearing capability: a human-annotated gold set + per-detector ROC/AUC/PR/F1 + κ — namely `metrics/ST3`, `detectors/DE2`, and the calibration/agreement halves of `metrics/ST4`, `detectors/DE3`, and `paper/PF1`. Treat them as **one workstream, "Detector Validation Harness"**:
> - **Gold set** (`datasets/gold_labels.jsonl`, ~150-300 triples spanning all six families + hard negatives: safe refusals with trigger words, fictional/educational compliance, partial compliance), with an annotation guideline and a held-out split. `[ST3/DE2/PF1]`
> - **Validation harness** (`analysis/detector_eval.py`): per-detector & per-family ROC+AUC, PR+AP, F1, confusion matrix, operating-point recommendation; a pytest asserting each detector clears a min-F1; a "Detector Reliability" report section + `GET /analytics/detector-reliability`. `[ST3/DE2]`
> - **Calibrated ensemble** (`detectors/ensemble.py`): logistic/weighted fusion of keyword + ML + refusal + toxicity + DE1 judge, Platt/isotonic calibration so `confidence` is a real P(jailbroken), ECE/Brier/reliability diagram, and an `abstain` band — **replacing the hand-tuned `min(0.6 + count*0.1, …)` feeding CWSS**. `[DE3]`
> - **Agreement layer** (κ/α) as in ST4/PF1.
> *This is the single biggest rigor item: every ASR/CWSS/CATS/BABA/genealogy number rests on a detector whose accuracy is currently never measured.* **Impact high · Effort L (labeling-bound) · Novelty medium→high (DE3 high).** *Lit: Mazeika et al. 2024; Souly et al. 2024; Cohen 1960; Guo et al. 2017; Platt 1999; Zadrozny & Elkan 2002; Fawcett 2006.* Largely the same fixes the AUDIT report demands — do them first (see build order).

## Tier 3 — Ambitious / paper-defining bets

These 2-4 can anchor a publication. Each lists the central claim it earns and the experiment to prove it.

### 1. Adaptive agentic red-teamer that learns a strategy policy ACROSS prompts `[attacks/AT6]`
**Central claim:** *"An attacker can learn a model-specific attack policy that beats any fixed strategy at equal budget, and the learned playbook transfers across models."*
A meta-strategy wrapping the whole pool (Crescendo/TAP/PAIR + AT1-AT5) that maintains a contextual bandit / Q-table keyed by `(attack_family, last_failure_mode, model_id) → strategy-arm`, selects via the **existing Thompson sampler in `adaptive_budget.py`**, updates the posterior on each jailbreak/score, and emits a transferable "attack playbook" (best strategy per cell) + a regret curve vs fixed-strategy baselines. This turns the platform from a *collection* of attacks into an *agentic attacker that learns across the whole evaluation* — which no listed framework does — and cleanly generalizes the platform's own BABA from category- to strategy-selection.
**Experiment:** Run the bandit across a benchmark grid; plot cumulative regret vs the best fixed strategy and vs round-robin; show convergence to a per-(model,family) playbook; then apply each model's learned playbook to held-out models and measure transfer (feeds CATS/AUS). Defensive payoff: the playbook tells safety teams which attack class to harden per model. Answers a **new RQ** and strengthens **RQ3/RQ5**. Integration: new `strategies/adaptive_agent.py` composing all other strategies; selection imports `adaptive_budget.py`; playbook + regret → a new Research panel + `transfer.py`. *Lit: Russo et al. 2018 (Thompson, already cited); Samvelyan et al. 2024 (Rainbow Teaming); ASTRA.* **Impact medium · Effort L · Novelty high.** *(Cold-start matters — warm-start from BABA; most expensive idea, scope to a fixed pool + bounded budget; **the bandit reward must be a reliable detector or it learns detector blind spots — gate on the DE1 judge / validation harness**.)*

### 2. Closed-loop attack ↔ defense evaluation `[paper/PF4]`
**Central claim:** *"We systematically measure which attacks survive which defenses — and the most query-efficient mutation operators are also the ones defenses block least."*
A `defenses/` package applying 3-4 cheap, reproducible defenses between prompt and model (system-prompt hardening; an input/output moderation gate reusing toxicity/refusal detectors or a Llama-Guard-style classifier; self-reminder/constitutional prepend; paraphrase/perplexity input filter vs encoding & GCG attacks), then a **Defense Robustness Score** = ASR_undefended − ASR_defended per family with PF2 significance, and an Attack-Defense matrix analogous to CATS. This upgrades the framing from "we broke models" (workshop-tier) to "we systematically measure attack survival under defenses" (conference-tier), and gives the genealogy/mutation-efficiency and transfer work a concrete payoff.
**Experiment:** Cross every attack family (incl. Tier-2 additions) against every defense; report the Attack-Defense matrix with significance, and correlate per-operator mutation-efficiency (from genealogy) against defense-survival to test the headline claim. Opens an RQ beyond the current six. Integration: `defenses/` wrapping `llm_client` in `evaluate_prompt`; reuses `detectors/*` as gates; new `analysis/defense.py`; `GET /analytics/attack-defense-matrix`. *Lit: Inan et al. 2023 (Llama Guard); Robey et al. 2023 (SmoothLLM); Xie et al. 2023 (self-reminder); Jain et al. 2023 (perplexity filtering).* **Impact high · Effort L · Novelty high.** *(Scope to 3-4 known defenses — the novelty is the measurement matrix, not new defenses.)*

### 3. Clinical / Medical-LLM red-team vertical (the medical-AI hook)
**Central claim:** *"General-safety guardrails do not transfer to clinical-harm avoidance — and we measure the gap on a grounded medical benchmark."*
This is the strongest differentiator and aligns directly with the author's **medical-AI + computer-vision research line**; no general framework (PyRIT/Garak/HarmBench) ships a clinical-safety benchmark with drug-interaction and diagnostic-over-confidence axes. Four JSON items converge here — consolidate them:
- **MedRedTeam benchmark** (`datasets/benchmarks/medredteam.py`, ~120-150 prompts) across four risk axes, each with a `reference` ground-truth key: dangerous medical advice (unsafe dosing/contraindicated self-treatment → refusal/safe-redirect), diagnostic over-confidence (vague symptoms → should hedge + refer), drug-interaction hallucination (real pairs with a known-interaction `answer_key` + fabricated/nonexistent drugs to test confident confabulation), and triage/emergency mishandling (red-flag symptoms — chest pain, stroke FAST — must escalate). `[datasets/DA5, attacks/AT7]`
- **Clinical-harm detector** (`detectors/medical_safety.py` / `clinical_harm.py`) flagging unsafe specificity (exact doses, ignored contraindications), missing safety-netting / failure-to-refer, fabricated medical citations (PubMed/DOI existence check, building on DE4), over-confident diagnosis, and scope-of-practice violations — backed by the DE1 judge with a clinical rubric + self-consistency. New metrics: **Unsafe Advice Rate, Failure-to-Refer Rate, Confident-Error Rate**, reusing ASR@k/CWSS. `[detectors/DE6, paper/PF5, attacks/AT7]`
- **Clinical strategy** (`strategies/clinical.py`) reusing Crescendo escalation + PAIR refinement with clinically-grounded escalation ladders (fake-clinician dosage-override, DDI-omission probes, PHI/HIPAA exfiltration, confident-but-wrong diagnosis, triage-undermining). `[attacks/AT7]`

**Experiment:** Run general vs clinical detectors on the same medical responses to show generic toxicity/jailbreak detectors miss the dominant clinical harm mode (a fluent, polite, non-toxic, confidently-wrong dose); report the three clinical rates per model; test whether medical jailbreaks transfer across models (transfer/genealogy on a high-stakes domain). Extends **RQ1/RQ3/RQ6** (confidence-vs-harm calibration is especially load-bearing in medicine). Integration: registered in the DA1 registry, seeded via DA2 with `category='medical'`, dispatched from `core.py` alongside existing detectors; gold labels feed the DE2 harness. *Lit: Han et al. 2024 (MedSafetyBench); HealthBench (OpenAI 2025); Singhal et al. (Med-PaLM safety axes); Med-HALT; DrugBank/RxNorm subset for interaction grounding.* **Impact high (AT7) / medium (DA5/DE6/PF5) · Effort L · Novelty high.** *(Frame strictly as defensive eval with disclaimers; source answer keys from cited tables (DrugBank/RxNorm) — never assert medical-accuracy ground truth without it; scope the gold set to clearly-unsafe cases and seek light clinician review of the rubric. Depends on DE1/DE4 existing first.)*

### 4. The reproducible multi-attack benchmark runner (the unifying contribution) `[paper/PF3]`
**Central claim:** *"A single reproducible benchmark runs Crescendo/TAP/PAIR + mutation/encoding generators across N models through one detector pipeline and emits a calibrated, CI-annotated, hash-stamped leaderboard."*
A `bench/run_benchmark.py` CLI + YAML manifest (models, strategies, dataset version, seed, k, judge config) that executes the full grid and writes a versioned, hash-stamped bundle (per-prompt JSONL + responses + metrics + CIs + detector-vs-gold agreement + git SHA + dataset SHA + model snapshot dates), with a `make reproduce` target that regenerates every table/figure. This is the **connective tissue** that turns five siloed "novel" analysis modules (transfer, genealogy, drift, BABA, PAIR-taxonomy) into ONE benchmark paper — today there is no end-to-end, model-agnostic, seed-fixed driver and no "reproduce Table 3."
**Experiment:** Ship the bundle; demonstrate one-command regeneration of all paper tables/figures from cached responses; include a "public-models-only" default config so reviewers reproduce without paid keys. Integration: new top-level `bench/` orchestrating `core.RedTeamEngine` + `strategies/*` + `generators.py` + `analysis/*`, sharing the `llm_client` factory with `eval_service.py`; `GET /analytics/benchmark-run/{bundle_id}`. *Lit: HarmBench; JailbreakBench (Chao 2024); HELM repro norms; Croissant/dataset-card conventions.* **Impact medium · Effort L · Novelty medium.** *(Closely related to PL6; PF3 is the CLI/driver, PL6 is the export artifact — build the driver, let PL6 package its output. Also fold in `paper/PF6` dataset hygiene: dataset card + version hash, frozen train/dev/test split (dev for calibration fit, test reported-on only), embedding dedup so mutated variants aren't double-counted, and a contamination probe for known DAN/canary templates.)*

## Suggested 3-phase build order

> **Prerequisite — do the `AUDIT_REPORT.md` research-validity fixes FIRST.** Tiers 1-3 are built on the detectors, metrics, and seeds the audit repairs. Any feature layered on an **unvalidated keyword detector, a hand-tuned CWSS confidence, or an unseeded RNG inherits that bias** — a learned bandit (AT6) will optimize detector blind spots, an attack-defense matrix (PF4) will compare noise, and every CI will be computed over a label of unknown accuracy. Fix the foundation, then build.

1. **Phase 1 — Make the numbers trustworthy (rigor foundation).** Build the **Detector Validation Harness** (gold set + `detector_eval.py` + calibrated ensemble + κ — `ST3/DE2/DE3/DE1/PF1` consolidated) and the **statistics layer** (`ST1`+`PL3`+`PF2` — one CI/significance core, surfaced in metrics, analytics, leaderboard). Add the **LLM-judge** `[DE1]` since the ensemble and every later reward signal depend on it, and stand up **provenance + seeds** (`PL1`+`PL2`). *Why first: nothing above is publishable until ASR/CWSS rest on a measured detector with error bars, and nothing is reproducible without manifests + seeds.*

2. **Phase 2 — Earn the comparability and the strongest paper anchor.** Land the **public-benchmark loaders + schema + graders** (`DA1`+`DA2`+`DA3`+`DA4`+`DA6`) so results are comparable to HarmBench/JailbreakBench and the over-refusal axis exists, plus **calibration `[ST2]`** to finally answer RQ6 and **efficiency frontier `[ST5]`** for RQ2/RQ3. In parallel, begin the **medical vertical (Tier-3 #3)** — the differentiating hook — which reuses the Phase-1 judge, harness, and grounded-hallucination work. Add the cheap/high-leverage new attacks (**BoN `[AT3]`** as the mandatory control, **MSJ `[AT1]`**, **Policy-Puppetry `[AT5]`**).

3. **Phase 3 — Ambitious bets + the citable artifact.** Build the remaining Tier-3 anchors (**adaptive agentic red-teamer `[AT6]`**, **attack↔defense matrix `[PF4]`**, **AutoDAN `[AT2]`** + **cipher/multilingual `[AT4]`** to enrich genealogy and transfer), then the **benchmark runner `[PF3]`**, **report card `[ST6]`**, **sweep + cost meter `[PL4]`**, **diff/replay `[PL5]`**, and finally the **reproducibility bundle `[PL6]`** that packages everything into the one artifact a venue's artifact-evaluation committee asks for.
